Aspen Restaurants

World-class dining at 8,000 feet

Aspen's culinary scene punches far above what you'd expect from a small mountain town. This is where celebrity chefs open outposts, where New York and LA restaurateurs test concepts, and where locals expect excellence because mediocrity doesn't survive here. The prices match the expectations—this isn't a cheap place to eat—but the quality is genuinely world-class.

What makes Aspen dining special is the combination of serious culinary ambition and mountain-town casualness. You can walk into a Michelin-worthy restaurant in ski boots, and nobody blinks. The seasonal rotation means menus change with the snow, and summer brings an entirely different energy with outdoor patios and food festivals.

Legendary Fine Dining

These restaurants would be celebrated in any major city. In Aspen, they're the anchors of a culinary scene that draws food lovers specifically to eat.

Matsuhisa Aspen

Downtown • Chef Nobu Matsuhisa

The original American Matsuhisa, opened in 1998, predates the global Nobu empire. Chef Nobu still visits regularly, and the Japanese-Peruvian fusion that launched a thousand imitators remains exceptional. The black cod miso is perfect, the sashimi is pristine, and the omakase experience is worth the splurge. Book well ahead in ski season—this is where everyone wants to eat.

Reserve: At least 2 weeks ahead during peak season.

$$$$ Icon

Element 47

The Little Nell Hotel

Named for silver (element 47, Aspen's origin story), this is the flagship restaurant of the Little Nell. The tasting menus showcase seasonal Colorado ingredients with French technique—elk, lamb, wild mushrooms—executed with precision. The wine program is one of the best in the Rockies, with a sommelier team that knows their cellar inside out. Special occasion dining at its finest.

For: Anniversaries, proposals, celebrating something big.

$$$$ Tasting Menu

Casa Tua

Downtown • Italian

The Aspen outpost of the Miami original, Casa Tua serves refined Italian in an intimate, residential-style setting. The pasta is handmade daily, the ingredients are flown in from Italy, and the atmosphere feels like a private dinner party. It's members-only in Miami; here, anyone with a reservation can experience it. The risotto and truffle dishes are exceptional.

Vibe: Like dining in a wealthy friend's beautiful home.

$$$$ Italian

Excellent Without the Formality

Great food without the fine-dining production. These spots let you dress down while eating up.

Meat & Cheese

Downtown Aspen

A farm-driven restaurant and market that sources from local ranches and producers. The name is literal—excellent charcuterie and cheese boards are the foundation—but the full menu extends to seasonal dishes that change regularly. The vibe is casual enough for lunch in ski clothes but refined enough for a proper dinner. Great for groups with the shared boards.

Don't miss: The local cheese board and daily special.

$$$ Farm-to-Table

White House Tavern

East Hopkins Ave

Comfort food elevated in a Victorian house setting. The menu is American classics done exceptionally well—the fried chicken is legendary, the burgers are proper, and the mac and cheese is what mac and cheese should be. It's cozy in winter with fireplaces going, and the patio is excellent in summer. Reservations are smart but not always necessary.

Must order: The fried chicken. No debate.

$$$ Comfort Food

Ajax Tavern

Base of Aspen Mountain

The aprés-ski institution at the base of Aspen Mountain. Everyone who skis Ajax ends their day here—or at least passes through. The truffle fries are famous, the burgers are excellent, and the patio has the best people-watching in town. It's not cheap, but the location and atmosphere are unmatched. Come for the scene as much as the food.

Essential: Truffle fries and a cold beer after skiing.

$$$ Après-Ski

Local Favorites

Where Aspen locals actually eat when they're not impressing visitors:

The Red Onion — The oldest bar in Aspen (since 1892), with better-than-expected food and a genuine local crowd. Mexican-leaning menu, strong margaritas, good vibes.

Poppycock's — Breakfast and lunch institution serving Aspen since 1972. Unpretentious, excellent omelets, and reasonable prices by local standards. Cash only.

Big Wrap — No-frills wrap and burrito spot that's been fueling ski mornings for decades. Fast, filling, affordable. The antidote to Aspen pricing.

L'Hostaria — Old-school Italian that feels like it's been there forever. Nothing fancy, just solid pasta and pizza in a cozy space. Good value for Aspen.

Dining Tips for Aspen

  • Book ahead in winter — December through March, every good restaurant is booked. Reserve 2-4 weeks ahead for fine dining, 1 week for casual.
  • Lunch is easier — Many fine dining spots serve lunch with the same kitchen at lower prices and easier reservations.
  • Prices are high — Budget $75-200+ per person for dinner at the top spots. Even casual places run $30-50 per person.
  • Summer is different — The pace slows, reservations are easier, and many restaurants open patios. Food festival season (June) brings special events.
  • Dress code is flexible — This is a mountain town. Smart casual works everywhere except the most formal spots.
