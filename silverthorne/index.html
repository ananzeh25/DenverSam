Silverthorne

Summit County's practical gateway

Silverthorne isn't the prettiest town in Summit County—that distinction goes to Breckenridge or Frisco. But it's the most practical. Sitting right at the bottom of the Eisenhower Tunnel on I-70, Silverthorne is the first stop into Summit County and the last stop before heading home. The outlet mall draws shoppers, the grocery stores serve vacation rentals, and the Blue River runs right through town.

For visitors, Silverthorne offers lower lodging prices than Breck, easy access to multiple ski areas, and surprisingly good outdoor recreation. It's also home to some excellent local businesses if you know where to look.

Shopping & Services

Outlets at Silverthorne

60+ stores

Colorado's highest-altitude outlet mall, featuring major brands like Nike, North Face, Columbia, Gap, and more. The outdoor gear shops are particularly useful—grab discounted ski layers or hiking gear. It's not glamorous shopping, but the deals are real. Best visited during sales or off-season; ski weekends get crowded.

Useful: Stock up on gear before hitting the slopes.

Shopping

Grocery & Essentials

Stock the rental

Silverthorne has the best grocery options in Summit County. City Market (Kroger) and Whole Foods cover most needs. Costco is a few exits east in Dillon. If you're staying in a rental, stop here first—prices are lower than resort town grocery stores, and selection is better. Liquor stores are well-stocked too.

Pro tip: Shop on arrival to avoid weekend traffic.

Practical

Outdoor Recreation

Silverthorne's outdoor access is underrated:

Blue River — The Blue River runs through town, offering gold medal trout fishing. The stretch through Silverthorne is wade-able and less crowded than famous sections downstream. You need a Colorado fishing license.

Dillon Reservoir — Just east of town, "Lake Dillon" offers sailing, kayaking, and SUP in summer. The Dillon Marina rents equipment. Sailing at 9,000 feet is a unique experience.

Eagles Nest Wilderness — Trailheads for Gore Range hikes are nearby. The Salmon Lake Trail and Willow Falls are accessible from the Silverthorne area.

Ski Access — Keystone is 10 minutes away, A-Basin 15 minutes, Breckenridge 20 minutes. Silverthorne is a budget-friendly base camp for multi-area trips.

Where to Eat

Sauce on the Blue — Italian-American with a nice Blue River patio. Good pasta, decent pizza, and a well-curated wine list.

The Mint Steakhouse — Historic steakhouse that's been here since the mining days. Prime cuts, classic sides, and genuine character.

Sunshine Café — Breakfast and lunch spot beloved by locals. Big portions, reasonable prices, and the kind of place where regulars know the staff.

Red Buffalo Coffee — Local coffee shop with good espresso and pastries. The drive-through is convenient on ski mornings.

Staying in Silverthorne

Silverthorne lodging is generally cheaper than Breckenridge and Keystone. Most options are vacation rentals and chain hotels rather than boutique properties. The trade-off: less charm, more value.

  • Best for: Budget-conscious families, multi-area ski trips, fishing trips
  • Drawback: No walkable downtown; you'll need a car
  • Book through: VRBO, Airbnb, or local property managers for condos and houses
