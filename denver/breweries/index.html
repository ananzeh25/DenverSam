Denver Breweries

The craft beer capital of America

Denver has more breweries than any other American city. That's not marketing hype—it's a fact that's been true since the craft beer renaissance began. The reasons are obvious once you're here: perfect water from the Rockies, a population obsessed with outdoor activities (and the beers that follow), and a culture that has supported local brewing since before "craft beer" was even a term.

With over 100 breweries in the metro area, no guide can cover them all. This one focuses on the best—places where the beer is genuinely excellent, not just "good for a brewery." Organized by neighborhood to help you plan a crawl, with honest takes on what makes each spot worth your time.

RiNo: Denver's Brewery Row

River North Art District packs more breweries per block than anywhere in Colorado. The industrial buildings, street art, and walkable layout make it perfect for hopping between taprooms. See our full RiNo Breweries Guide for complete coverage, but here are the highlights:

Great Divide Barrel Bar

3403 Brighton Blvd

One of Denver's original craft breweries, Great Divide now has two locations. The RiNo Barrel Bar is where they pour rare barrel-aged beers you won't find anywhere else. The Yeti Imperial Stout variations are legendary—some aged in bourbon barrels, some in whiskey, all incredible. The space is industrial-chic with a huge patio. Essential Denver drinking.

Must try: Whatever barrel-aged special is on tap.

Bierstadt Lagerhaus

2875 Blake St

German-style lagers brewed with obsessive attention to detail. The slow-pour pilsner is famous—poured over 5 minutes into proper glassware, the way it's done in Czech beer halls. The result is the best pilsner in Colorado, and probably America. Don't come here for IPAs; come here to understand why lagers ruled the world before craft beer happened. The pretzel with beer cheese is mandatory.

Order: The Slow Pour Pils. Wait for it. Worth it.

Our Mutual Friend

2810 Larimer St

The polar opposite of Bierstadt—wild ales, farmhouse saisons, and funky sours brewed with local ingredients. OMF feels like a neighborhood bar that happens to make exceptional beer. The space is intimate, the dog-friendly patio is great, and the tap list changes constantly. If you like Belgian-influenced brewing and sours, this is your spot.

Best for: Sour beer lovers and people tired of IPAs.

Ratio Beerworks

2920 Larimer St

RiNo's most versatile brewery covers every style well. Their IPAs are crisp and balanced, the lagers are clean, and they rotate creative seasonals. The huge outdoor space with picnic tables and fire pits makes it perfect for groups. Food trucks rotate daily. One of the few spots where everyone in your crew will find something they love.

Best for: Groups with varied tastes and outdoor hangs.

Odell Brewing Denver

2945 Larimer St

Fort Collins institution's Denver outpost brings decades of brewing expertise to RiNo. The 90 Shilling amber ale is a Colorado classic, and their IPA lineup stays consistently excellent. The space is polished with great design and a solid food menu. Perfect for visitors who want reliable quality without hunting for hidden gems.

Must try: 90 Shilling - the Colorado amber standard.

Beyond RiNo: Neighborhood Favorites

Comrade Brewing

7667 E Iliff Ave • Southeast Denver

A bit of a drive from downtown, but beer nerds make the pilgrimage for some of Colorado's best IPAs. Comrade has won more Great American Beer Festival medals than almost anyone, and their Superpower IPA is a desert island beer for many locals. The taproom is no-frills—you come for the beer, not the ambiance. Worth the trip if hops are your thing.

Awards: More GABF medals than you can count.

$$ Award Winner

TRVE Brewing

227 Broadway • Baker

Metal-themed brewery that takes its sour beer as seriously as its doom metal soundtrack. The beers are excellent—complex wild ales, saisons, and sours fermented in their own coolship. The vibe is exactly what you'd expect: dark, loud, and not for everyone. But if you appreciate both good beer and heavy music, TRVE is a temple. The taproom has a deliberately dark aesthetic.

Vibe: Metal fans and sour beer geeks unite.

$$ Metal + Sours

Cerebral Brewing

1477 Monroe St • Congress Park

One of Denver's newer stars, Cerebral focuses on precision and creativity. Their hazy IPAs are some of the best in town, but they also nail stouts, lagers, and experimental styles. The taproom is clean and modern, and they often collaborate with other breweries. The can releases draw lines—check their socials for drop dates. Great beer for people who pay attention to beer.

Watch for: Limited can releases and collabs.

The Classics: Denver's OGs

Wynkoop Brewing — Denver's first craft brewery, opened in 1988 in LoDo. Still serving solid beer in a historic building with pool tables and pub food. A piece of Colorado craft beer history.

Denver Beer Co — Two taprooms serving approachable, crowd-pleasing beers. The Platte Street location has a huge patio; the Olde Town Arvada spot is family-friendly. Good for groups with mixed beer preferences.

Crooked Stave — Brett and sour specialists with a devoted following. Their taproom at The Source food hall is a good entry point to American wild ales. Not for the faint of palate.

Brewery Crawl Tips

  • RiNo is walkable — You can hit 6-8 breweries on foot along Larimer/Blake streets.
  • Use rideshare — Denver's breweries are spread out. Don't drive if you're doing more than 2-3 stops.
  • Food trucks are common — Many breweries don't have kitchens but allow outside food or have trucks parked outside.
  • Dogs are welcome — Most patios are dog-friendly. Check individual policies for indoor spaces.
  • Flights exist — Sample before committing to a full pour. Most places offer 4-6 tasters.
  • GABF weekend gets crazy — The Great American Beer Festival (October) brings massive crowds. Plan accordingly or embrace the chaos.
