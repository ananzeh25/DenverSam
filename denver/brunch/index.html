Denver Brunch

Weekend mornings done right

Denver takes brunch seriously. Maybe it's the late weekend sunrise, the recovery from Friday's brewery hopping, or the fact that Colorado's best activities happen after 10am anyway—whatever the reason, the city has developed one of the best brunch scenes in the country. From classic greasy spoons to chef-driven breakfast experiences, you'll find something for every craving and budget.

This guide covers spots that locals actually frequent—places where the food justifies the wait, the drinks are properly made, and the atmosphere makes lingering feel natural. Skip the tourist traps and eat where Denver eats.

The Classics: Denver Institutions

These spots have earned their reputation over years of consistently excellent breakfast. They get crowded, but they're worth it.

Snooze, an A.M. Eatery

Multiple Locations • Born in Denver

The Denver brunch phenomenon that spawned a national chain. Snooze started in LoDo in 2006 and perfected the modern brunch formula: creative pancake flights, eggs Benedict variations, and cocktails that don't feel like an afterthought. The original Larimer Street location has the most character, but all locations maintain quality. Expect a wait on weekends—put your name in and grab coffee nearby.

Must try: Pineapple Upside Down Pancakes or the OMG French Toast.

$$ Expect a Wait

Denver Biscuit Company

Colfax & South Broadway

Massive, buttery biscuits are the foundation of everything here. The sandwiches are comically large—each could feed two—and made with biscuits baked fresh throughout the day. The gravy is proper Southern-style, and the fried chicken is genuinely excellent. It's casual and often crowded, but worth it for carb lovers. The Colfax location has more space; SoBo is smaller but charming.

Order: The Franklin (fried chicken, egg, bacon, honey butter, cheese).

$$ Southern Comfort

Steuben's

Uptown

Upscale comfort food in a retro diner setting. Steuben's brunch leans toward refined American classics—eggs done every way, chicken and waffles that set the local standard, and proper Bloody Marys. The room has vintage charm with booths and counter seating. It's popular but moves quickly. Their huevos rancheros are surprisingly good for a place known for Americana.

Pro tip: The patio is dog-friendly and lovely in warm weather.

$$ Retro Vibe

Jelly

Capitol Hill

A Capitol Hill staple that takes brunch basics and executes them flawlessly. Everything is house-made, from the preserves to the hot sauce. The menu covers classic breakfast territory—eggs, pancakes, hash—but with better ingredients and more care than chain spots. The space is small and waits can be long, but they serve coffee outside while you wait. Cash and card accepted.

Don't skip: Whatever pancake special is running that week.

$$ Housemade Everything

Boozy Brunch Specialists

When the point is as much about the drinks as the food, these spots deliver both.

Linger

LoHi • Rooftop Bar

Housed in a converted mortuary (yes, really), Linger offers "global comfort food" with a killer rooftop and strong cocktail program. The brunch menu pulls from multiple cuisines—Korean fried chicken, chilaquiles, duck fat potatoes—executed with more precision than most. The rooftop with downtown views is the real draw. Reservations strongly recommended.

Drink: The Bloody Mary bar lets you build your own.

$$$ Rooftop Views

The Nickel

Hotel Teatro • Downtown

A beautiful restaurant in the Hotel Teatro serving elevated American brunch. The space is stunning—high ceilings, art deco touches—and the food matches. Think lobster eggs benedict, duck confit hash, and pastries from the in-house bakery. Cocktails are properly made by people who know what they're doing. It's pricier than neighborhood spots but feels special.

For: Celebrating something or impressing someone.

$$$ Special Occasion

Neighborhood Gems

These spots are beloved by their neighborhoods—less famous citywide but excellent.

Onefold (Capitol Hill) — Locally-sourced breakfast tacos and rice bowls in a bright, minimalist space. Everything is fresh, portions are generous, and the green chili is addictive. No-frills counter service keeps prices reasonable.

Sassafras (Five Points) — Southern comfort food with New Orleans flair. The beignets are proper, the shrimp and grits are rich, and the patio is one of Denver's most pleasant. Weekend waits are common but move quickly.

Syrup (Multiple locations) — Straightforward breakfast done right. Nothing revolutionary, just excellent pancakes, eggs, and bacon in clean, comfortable spaces. Good for groups and families.

Root Down (LoHi) — Farm-to-table brunch in a converted gas station. The menu changes seasonally, accommodates dietary restrictions well, and the drinks are excellent. Beautiful patio in summer.

Brunch Tips from Locals

  • Go early or late — Peak brunch is 10am-noon. Arrive at 9am or after 1pm to skip the worst waits.
  • Weekdays win — Most brunch spots serve Tuesday-Sunday. Tuesday brunch has no wait and the same food.
  • Check apps — Many spots use Yelp Waitlist or Nowait. Put your name in from home.
  • Bottomless deals vary — Some are $20 mimosas for 2 hours, some are $30 for unlimited everything. Ask what's included.
  • Tipping is standard — 18-20% on full check including drinks. Brunch service is demanding.
