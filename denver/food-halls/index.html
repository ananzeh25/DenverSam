Denver Food Halls

Multiple vendors, one perfect meal

Denver embraced the food hall concept early and well. The model works perfectly here: groups with different cravings, craft beverage programs to tie it together, and Instagram-friendly industrial spaces. The city now has multiple excellent options, each with its own personality and vendor mix.

Food halls solve the "where should we eat" problem—everyone picks what they want, you share a table, everyone's happy. Here's where to go and what to get.

The Main Players

Avanti Food & Beverage

LoHi

The one that started it all. Avanti calls itself a "collective eatery," and the concept launched Denver's food hall obsession. Multiple vendor stalls rotate, offering everything from ramen to tacos to Mediterranean. The rooftop has panoramic mountain views—one of the best sunset spots in the city. Drinks are centralized at the bar, making it easy to browse vendors while sipping.

Go for: The rooftop at sunset. Stay for the food variety.

$$ Rooftop Views
Address: 3200 Pecos St, Denver, CO 80211
Hours: Sun-Thu 11am-10pm, Fri-Sat 11am-11pm
Parking: Street parking, paid lot nearby
Vendors: 7+ rotating food vendors

Stanley Marketplace

Aurora (Stapleton area)

Denver's largest food hall, housed in a converted aviation manufacturing plant from the 1950s. The scale is impressive—over 50 vendors including restaurants, shops, fitness studios, and a music venue. The food is excellent: Annette for seasonal American, Mondo Market for provisions, Rosenberg's for bagels. Easy parking and family-friendly. Could spend half a day here.

Highlights: Annette, Rosenberg's Bagels, Denver Biscuit Co.

$$ Family Friendly
Address: 2501 Dallas St, Aurora, CO 80010
Hours: Daily 8am-10pm (varies by vendor)
Parking: Free lot on-site
Vendors: 50+ food and retail vendors
Get Directions View Website

The Source

RiNo

A curated market in a converted 1880s foundry. The Source skews more upscale than typical food halls—think artisan rather than casual. Acorn remains a destination restaurant. Crooked Stave pours excellent sours. Mondo Market is perfect for provisions. The expansion (Source Hotel) added more vendors and a rooftop bar. More boutique vibes than the others.

Best for: Date night browsing, special ingredients, quality drinks.

$$$ Upscale
Address: 3350 Brighton Blvd, Denver, CO 80216
Hours: Daily 7am-10pm (varies by vendor)
Parking: Street parking, paid lots nearby
Vendors: 15+ artisan vendors
Get Directions View Website

Denver Central Market

RiNo

A true European-style market. Each vendor is permanent, not rotating—they're invested in building something lasting. Vero Italian for pasta, Curio for cocktails, Izzio Bakery for bread, High Point Creamery for ice cream. The quality is consistently high. Great for grabbing provisions or sitting down for a proper meal. Central bar ties everything together.

Must try: Pasta from Vero, bread from Izzio, ice cream from High Point.

$$ European Style
Address: 2669 Larimer St, Denver, CO 80205
Hours: Daily 8am-9pm
Parking: Street parking, nearby garages
Vendors: 10+ permanent vendors

Also Worth Visiting

Zeppelin Station (RiNo) — More casual than The Source, with good food stalls and a nice patio. Connected to the light rail.

Edgewater Public Market (Edgewater) — Neighborhood-scale food hall with a brewery, whiskey bar, and local vendors. Less touristy than the RiNo options.

Dairy Block (Downtown) — Boutique alley with shops, restaurants, and a speakeasy. More curated shopping than food hall, but worth exploring.

Food Hall Tips

  • Arrive early or late — Peak hours (12-1pm, 6-8pm) mean waiting for tables. Off-peak is easier.
  • Scout first — Walk the whole space before ordering. Know your options.
  • Share a table — It's expected. Don't feel weird about it.
  • Centralized bars help — Order drinks at the bar, then get food. Easier coordination.
  • Parking varies — Stanley has a lot. RiNo spots need street parking or rideshare.
