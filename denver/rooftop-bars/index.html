Denver Rooftop Bars

Drinks with a view of the Mile High skyline

Denver's 300+ days of sunshine make rooftop drinking a near year-round activity. From downtown hotel bars with skyline views to neighborhood spots where you can watch the sun set behind the Rockies, the city has embraced elevated drinking in every sense. The best rooftops offer more than views—they have cocktail programs worth the climb and atmospheres that range from upscale lounges to casual beer gardens.

Here's where to find Denver's best rooftop experiences, organized by vibe and what you're looking for.

The Iconic Views

These are the spots people picture when they think "Denver rooftop bar"—serious views, proper cocktails, and usually a dress code above jeans and flip-flops.

54thirty

Downtown • Le Méridien Denver

The name says it: 5,430 feet elevation makes this Denver's highest open-air rooftop bar. The 360-degree views span from downtown towers to the Rocky Mountains, and on clear days you can see from Pikes Peak to Longs Peak. The cocktail program is crafted and seasonal, with prices to match the altitude. It gets packed at sunset, so arrive early for a good spot. Open seasonally, roughly April through October.

Best time: 30 minutes before sunset for the full light show.

$$$ Seasonal

The Red Barber

Downtown • The Ramble Hotel

A rooftop bar operated by the legendary Death & Co team, which tells you everything about the cocktails. The drinks here are world-class—complex, well-balanced, and made with care. The space is stylish with comfortable seating and heaters for cooler evenings. Views face downtown and the mountains. It's more intimate than 54thirty, with a focus on the drink experience rather than the party scene.

For: People who care about craft cocktails as much as views.

$$$ Year-Round

Peaks Lounge

Downtown • Hyatt Regency

On the 27th floor of the Hyatt Regency, Peaks has been serving Denver's high-altitude views since long before rooftop bars were trendy. Floor-to-ceiling windows provide panoramic views of the Rockies, and unlike open-air spots, it's comfortable year-round. The cocktail menu is solid, the wine list extensive, and the atmosphere is upscale without being stuffy. A good choice for winter when outdoor rooftops close.

Note: Indoor space means it works in any season or weather.

$$$ Indoor

Casual & Neighborhood Rooftops

Not every rooftop drink needs to be a production. These spots offer good views and good drinks without the scene.

Linger Rooftop

LoHi

Atop the converted mortuary that houses Linger restaurant, this rooftop patio offers excellent downtown skyline views without downtown prices. It's connected to the restaurant, so you can get proper food along with your drinks. The vibe is more relaxed than the hotel rooftops—you won't feel underdressed in casual clothes. Great for groups who want to eat and drink.

Order: The Asian-inspired small plates and a boozy slushie.

$$ Food Available

Federales

LoDo

A Mexican-themed bar with a massive rooftop patio in the heart of LoDo. It's more party atmosphere than sophisticated lounge—think margaritas, shot skis, and groups celebrating. The frozen drinks are actually decent, and the tacos are good enough. Views of downtown and great people-watching. Goes late and gets loud on weekends. If that's your vibe, it delivers.

Best for: Birthday parties, bachelorettes, pre-game drinks.

$$ Party Vibe

Avanti Food & Beverage

LoHi

A food hall with a fantastic rooftop patio. Multiple food vendors mean everyone in your group can eat what they want, and the bar serves craft beer, cocktails, and wine. The rooftop has incredible mountain views—the Front Range stretches out to the west. It's casual and family-friendly during the day, more of a scene at night. One of Denver's best spots for sunset drinks.

Tip: Arrive 45 minutes before sunset for rooftop seating.

$$ Food Hall

Rooftop Tips

  • Check the season — Most open-air rooftops close November-March. Call ahead if the weather's borderline.
  • Arrive early for sunset — The best spots fill up 30-45 minutes before sunset. You can't reserve rooftop tables at most places.
  • Layer up — Even in summer, evenings get cool at altitude. Bring a jacket.
  • Know the dress code — Hotel rooftops (54thirty, Red Barber) lean upscale casual. Neighborhood spots don't care.
  • Prices are higher — You're paying for the view. Budget $15-20 per cocktail at the fancy spots.
