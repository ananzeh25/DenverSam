Denver Tacos

From street carts to sit-down taquerias

Denver's taco scene reflects the city's large Mexican and Central American communities—authentic, diverse, and often found in strip malls and parking lots rather than trendy neighborhoods. This is a city where a $2 street taco can outclass a $6 "artisanal" one, and where the best finds require ignoring Google ratings in favor of lines and word-of-mouth.

This guide covers the spectrum: no-frills taquerias where Spanish is the primary language, modern taco concepts that lean creative, and the taco trucks that fuel late nights and early mornings. All tested, all worth your time.

Authentic Taquerias

The real deal—family-owned spots serving tacos like you'd find in Mexico City, Jalisco, or wherever the family is from.

Tacos Selene

Federal Blvd • Multiple Locations

The taqueria that built Denver's street taco reputation. What started as a truck is now multiple locations, but the quality hasn't slipped. Tiny corn tortillas, perfectly seasoned meats, proper cilantro and onion. The al pastor is excellent, the suadero is the move, and at $2-3 a taco, you can try everything. The Federal Blvd location has the most character.

Order: Suadero, al pastor, lengua. Three each.

$ Essential
Address: 3561 W 38th Ave, Denver, CO 80211
Phone: (303) 477-1625
Hours: 9am–11pm daily
Rating: ★★★★★ 4.7 (2,100+ reviews)

La Calle Taqueria

Santa Fe Drive

A slightly more modern space serving traditional Mexico City-style street food. The tacos are excellent—hand-pressed tortillas, quality meats—but they also do proper quesadillas, huaraches, and sopes. The birria is outstanding when they have it. More comfortable than the true holes-in-the-wall, but no less authentic in execution.

Try: The handmade blue corn tortillas.

$ DF Style
Address: 2715 Santa Fe Dr, Denver, CO 80211
Phone: (303) 954-8266
Hours: 11am–9pm daily
Rating: ★★★★½ 4.6 (850+ reviews)

Tacos Tequila Whiskey

RiNo & LoDo

A chef-driven take on Mexican street food that manages to be creative without losing authenticity. The duck carnitas taco is signature—rich, crispy, excellent. They do things with mole and salsas that show real culinary skill. The bar program is strong too—this is a full restaurant experience disguised as a taco joint. RiNo location has the best vibe.

Signature: Duck carnitas, Al pastor, and their housemade salsas.

Late Night & Taco Trucks

After midnight, the taco trucks take over. These are the spots that fuel the bar crowd, the shift workers, and anyone who knows that the best tacos often come from a window.

El Taco de Mexico

Santa Fe Dr • Since 1985

The green chili burrito here is legendary—smothered, massive, and available into the night. A counter-service institution feeding Denver's Mexican food cravings for 40+ years.

Order: Green chili smothered burrito.

$ Institution
Address: 714 Santa Fe Dr, Denver, CO 80204
Phone: (303) 623-3926
Hours: 6am–10pm daily

Modern Taco Joints

Creative takes on tacos for when you want something different—or when you need more seating and drinks than a taqueria offers.

Los Chingones

RiNo

A full-service Mexican restaurant with excellent tacos and an even better bar. The margaritas are proper (no sour mix in sight), the guacamole is made tableside, and the tacos are creative without being gimmicky. It's a party atmosphere—loud, colorful, fun. Good for groups who want tacos and tequila in equal measure.

For: Groups, birthdays, tequila tastings.

$$ Full Bar
Address: 2463 Larimer St, Denver, CO 80205
Phone: (720) 657-9476
Hours: 11am–10pm Sun–Thu, 11am–11pm Fri–Sat
Rating: ★★★★ 4.3 (1,800+ reviews)

Torchy's Tacos

Multiple Locations

The Austin import that's become a Denver staple. Not authentic Mexican—these are Tex-Mex creative tacos with big flavors. The Trailer Park (fried chicken, green chilis) and Dirty Sanchez (scrambled eggs, bacon, poblano peppers) are crowd favorites. It's a chain, but a good one. Consistent, flavorful, and the queso is addictive.

Secret menu: Ask for items "trashy" to add queso and jalapeños.

Taco Tips

  • Federal Blvd is the strip — More authentic Mexican food per mile than anywhere in Denver. Just drive and stop where it looks busy.
  • Cash is king — Many of the best taquerias are cash-only. Hit an ATM first.
  • Order in Spanish if you can — It's appreciated and sometimes gets you access to specials not on the English menu.
  • Green or red salsa? — Red is usually milder; green can go either way. Ask "¿Pica?" if you're unsure.
  • Try the offal — Lengua (tongue), tripas (intestine), cabeza (head meat) are often the best tacos on the menu. Don't miss them.
