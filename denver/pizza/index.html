Denver Pizza

Every style, every neighborhood

Denver's pizza scene has grown up. Where there was once only chains and average neighborhood spots, there's now everything from legitimate Neapolitan pizzerias with imported ovens to Detroit-style squares that stand up to the Motor City originals. Whether you want a quick slice, a whole pie for the table, or a dining experience built around pizza, Denver delivers.

This guide covers the best by style—because what you're craving matters more than what's closest. Each spot has been tested repeatedly by locals who take pizza seriously.

New York Style

For the transplants who miss real slices and anyone who appreciates a proper fold.

Famous Original J's

Downtown Denver

The closest Denver gets to a NYC slice joint. The pizza is thin, foldable, and served by the slice until late. The crust has that proper NY char, the cheese ratio is right, and the atmosphere is no-frills. It's not trying to be anything other than good New York pizza, and it succeeds. Essential for late-night downtown eats.

Order: Plain cheese or pepperoni. Keep it classic.

$ Late Night

Sexy Pizza

Multiple Locations

Don't let the name fool you—this is serious pizza. Each location is unique, but all serve properly thin NY-style slices with creative topping options. The dough is made in-house, the sauce is tangy, and the portions are generous. Some locations have great patios. A Denver original that's earned its reputation.

Try: The Margherita or any daily special.

$$ Local Chain

Neapolitan

Blistered crusts, fresh mozzarella, 90-second cook times—the Italian standard done right.

Cart-Driver

RiNo

Built inside shipping containers in RiNo, Cart-Driver serves some of Denver's best Neapolitan pizza. The wood-fired oven produces perfectly blistered, slightly charred crusts with that signature chew. The menu is focused—a handful of pizzas, some salads, oysters on the half shell—and everything is excellent. The outdoor space fills up fast; go early or late.

Must try: The Burrata pizza is a thing of beauty.

$$ Wood-Fired

Pizzeria Locale

RiNo & DTC

A collaboration between Frasca Food & Wine (Boulder's fine-dining gem) and Chipotle's founder—an unusual pedigree that produces excellent pizza. The Neapolitan pies are proper: imported Italian flour, San Marzano tomatoes, custom-built ovens. Fast-casual service keeps prices reasonable. The Margherita is textbook; the seasonal specials are worth exploring.

Great for: Quick, quality pizza without the wait.

$$ Fast Casual

Detroit Style

The pan pizza that's taking over—thick, airy, with crispy cheese edges.

Blue Pan Pizza

West Highland & Congress Park

The Detroit-style standard in Denver. Blue Pan uses proper blue steel pans, Wisconsin brick cheese, and a 72-hour fermented dough that produces the signature airy, crispy-edged square slices. The cheese-to-edge ratio is perfect—each corner piece is pure caramelized cheese. They've won national awards and earned them. Worth any wait.

Essential: Get the corner pieces—the "frico" cheese edges are the point.

$$ Award Winner

Diorio's Pizza

Multiple Locations

Detroit-style with a Colorado twist. The squares are thick, the cheese edges are properly crispy, and the topping combinations lean creative. They also do solid NY-style by the slice. The locations are neighborhood joints—casual, family-friendly, good for takeout or dining in. Consistent quality across all spots.

Try: The pepperoni cup-and-char Detroit style.

$$ Neighborhood

More Great Pizza

Redeemer Pizza (Englewood) — A newcomer making some of Denver's best pies. Creative toppings, perfect crusts, and a small but mighty menu. Worth the drive south.

Brava! Pizzeria Della Strada (Multiple) — Wood-fired Roman-style pizza—thin, crispy, excellent. The Diavola with spicy salami is a standout.

Proto's Pizzeria Napoletana (Boulder, Longmont, Denver) — Reliable Neapolitan with a casual vibe. Great for families and groups.

Fat Sully's (Multiple) — Giant NY-style slices for the late-night crowd. More about quantity than refinement, but sometimes that's exactly right.

Pizza Tips

  • Detroit style takes time — The dough needs to proof. Expect 20-30 minute waits at popular spots. Worth it.
  • Neapolitan is quick — 90-second cook times mean fast service. Great for lunches.
  • Call ahead for whole pies — Especially on weekends. Many places let you order ahead.
  • Late night options exist — Famous Original J's and Fat Sully's are your best bets after midnight.
  • Don't sleep on lunch specials — Many pizzerias offer deals at lunch that disappear at dinner.
