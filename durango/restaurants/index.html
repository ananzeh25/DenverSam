Durango Restaurants

Southwest Colorado's culinary heart

Durango doesn't have the glitz of Aspen or the crowds of Vail, and that's exactly the point. This college town at 6,500 feet has developed a food scene that reflects its character: unpretentious, creative, and built around outdoor lifestyle. Historic Main Avenue is the spine—Victorian buildings now house everything from craft breweries to farm-to-table restaurants.

What makes Durango special is the value. You'll eat better for less here than at any other Colorado mountain town. The train tourists (Durango & Silverton Railroad) keep restaurants busy, but locals keep them honest. Expect friendly service, generous portions, and genuine pride in the food.

Local Favorites

Eolus

Main Avenue

Durango's most celebrated restaurant, named for one of the famous 14ers visible from town. The menu showcases Colorado ingredients—elk, trout, seasonal vegetables—prepared with technique that belies the casual mountain setting. The bar program is serious, and the wine list punches above its weight. This is where locals bring visitors to impress.

Don't miss: The elk preparation, whatever it is that night.

$$$ Fine Dining

Steamworks Brewing

Main Avenue

A Durango institution since 1996. Steamworks is a brewpub that takes both the beer and the food seriously. House beers are excellent—the Colorado Kolsch is a local staple—and the menu runs from pub classics to more ambitious plates. The historic building, located in a former car dealership, has character. Everyone ends up here at some point.

Order: Colorado Kolsch and the green chili burger.

$$ Brewpub

Chimayo Stone Fired Kitchen

Main Avenue

Southwestern flavors meet stone-fired cooking. The namesake comes from Chimayo, New Mexico, and that influence shows throughout the menu—red and green chiles, blue corn, regional preparations. The wood-fired oven produces excellent pizzas and roasted dishes. The atmosphere is warm and inviting, the margaritas are strong, and the patio is lovely in summer.

Perfect for: Groups and anyone craving Southwest flavors.

$$ Southwest

Ore House

Main Avenue

Old-school steakhouse that's been serving Durango since the 1970s. Prime cuts, traditional preparations, proper martinis—no reinvention, just consistent excellence. The dark wood interior feels appropriately Western, and the service is professional without being stuffy. Come hungry; portions are generous. A celebration-worthy splurge.

Classic choice: Filet or ribeye, baked potato, Caesar salad.

$$$ Steakhouse

Casual Eats

East by Southwest — Creative Asian-fusion that shouldn't work in southwest Colorado but absolutely does. The sushi is fresh (surprisingly), the ramen is warming, and the atmosphere is fun.

Nini's Taqueria — Legit Mexican food in a casual setting. The tacos are excellent, the burritos are massive, and the salsa bar is well-stocked. Cash-friendly pricing.

Jean-Pierre Bakery — French bakery serving breakfast and lunch. The croissants are proper, the coffee is strong, and the quiche is worth the early wake-up.

Oscar's Café — Big breakfast portions, solid diner food, quick service. Where locals go for a no-fuss morning meal before hitting the trail.

Breweries Worth Visiting

Durango punches above its weight in craft beer. Beyond Steamworks:

Ska Brewing — The local giant, known for canned beers sold statewide. The taproom has a party atmosphere and excellent Mexican Lager.

Carver Brewing — Another Durango pioneer, in a beautiful old building. More refined beers and a solid food menu.

Animas Brewing — Small-batch, experimental brews in a neighborhood setting. Where local beer nerds congregate.
