Vail Hiking

Summer in the Gore Range

When the snow melts, Vail transforms. The ski runs become wildflower meadows, the lifts access alpine trails, and the hiking rivals anywhere in Colorado. The Gore Range provides dramatic scenery—jagged peaks, alpine lakes, and views that stretch to the Continental Divide. Whether you want an easy nature walk or a challenging summit attempt, Vail delivers.

This guide covers the best trails by difficulty, with honest assessments of what to expect and tips for making the most of each hike.

Easy & Family-Friendly

Gore Creek Trail

4-10 miles • Flat • Creekside

A paved path that runs through Vail Village along Gore Creek. Go as far as you want and turn around—the full trail extends well beyond town. Perfect for families, strollers, and anyone wanting a mellow walk with mountain views. Connects to restaurants in the village for an easy post-hike meal.

Best for: Easy morning walk, families with kids.

Easy Paved

Bighorn Creek Trail

3 miles • Moderate • Wildlife

A gentle trail through aspen groves and meadows, with good chances of seeing bighorn sheep (hence the name). The elevation gain is minimal, making it accessible for most fitness levels. Early morning or evening offers the best wildlife viewing. Connects to longer trails if you want to extend.

Wildlife tip: Bring binoculars and go early.

Easy-Moderate Wildlife

Moderate Day Hikes

Booth Falls

4.4 miles RT • 1,100' gain • Waterfall

The most popular hike in Vail for good reason. The trail climbs steadily through aspen forests to a stunning 60-foot waterfall. Best in early summer when snowmelt maximizes the falls. Gets crowded—start early (before 8am) to beat the rush and secure parking. The effort-to-reward ratio is excellent.

Start early: Trailhead parking fills by 9am in summer.

Moderate Most Popular

Piney River Falls

6 miles RT • 600' gain • Alpine Lake

Less crowded than Booth Falls with equally impressive scenery. The trail passes Piney Lake (stunning in its own right) before continuing to the falls. The longer drive to the trailhead filters out some crowds. Bring a picnic—there are beautiful spots near the lake. A full morning or half-day commitment.

Bonus: Piney Lake alone is worth the drive.

Moderate Less Crowded

Challenging Adventures

For experienced hikers looking for bigger objectives:

Mount of the Holy Cross (14,009') — Colorado's most iconic 14er, with a snow cross visible on its face. The standard route is long (12+ miles) and strenuous (5,600' gain), but the views are transcendent. Start before dawn; be off the summit by noon for weather. Technical experience recommended.

Booth Lake (8 miles RT, 3,000' gain) — Continue past Booth Falls to this stunning alpine lake in a glacial cirque. The trail steepens significantly above the falls. Bring layers; it's cold at 11,500' even in summer.

Gore Range Trail — Multi-day backpacking along the spine of the Gore Range. Permits required for wilderness camping. Remote, rugged, and rewarding.

Hiking Tips for Vail

  • Start early — Afternoon thunderstorms are common June-August. Be below treeline by 1pm.
  • Altitude matters — Vail sits at 8,150'. Give yourself time to acclimate before tackling big elevation gains.
  • Bring layers — Mornings are cool, afternoons warm, storms cold. Be prepared for all conditions.
  • Pack water — More than you think you need. The dry mountain air dehydrates quickly.
  • Parking fills early — Popular trailheads (Booth Falls especially) are full by 9am on summer weekends.
  • Check conditions — Snow lingers at higher elevations into July. Trail conditions vary by year.
