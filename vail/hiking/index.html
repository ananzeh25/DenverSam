Vail Hiking

Summer in the Gore Range

When the snow melts, Vail transforms. The ski runs become wildflower meadows, the lifts access alpine trails, and the hiking rivals anywhere in Colorado. The Gore Range provides dramatic scenery—jagged peaks, alpine lakes, and views that stretch to the Continental Divide. Whether you want an easy nature walk or a challenging summit attempt, Vail delivers.

This guide covers the best trails by difficulty, with honest assessments of what to expect and tips for making the most of each hike.

Easy & Family-Friendly

Gore Creek Trail

4-10 miles • Flat • Creekside

Paved path through Vail Village along Gore Creek. Go as far as you want—the full trail extends well beyond town. Perfect for families, strollers, and anyone wanting a mellow walk with mountain views.

Easy Paved
Trailhead: Ford Park, Vail Village
Parking: Free at Ford Park (large lot)
Distance: Variable, up to 10+ miles
Season: Year-round (paved)

Bighorn Creek Trail

3 miles • Moderate • Wildlife

Gentle trail through aspen groves and meadows with good chances of seeing bighorn sheep. Minimal elevation gain. Early morning or evening best for wildlife viewing.

Easy-Moderate Wildlife
Trailhead: Bighorn Creek Trailhead, Minturn
Parking: Free, small roadside lot
Distance: 3 miles round trip
Season: June - October

Moderate Day Hikes

Booth Falls

4.4 miles RT • 1,100' gain • Waterfall

Most popular hike in Vail. Climbs through aspen forests to a stunning 60-foot waterfall. Best in early summer when snowmelt maximizes the falls. Start before 8am to beat crowds and secure parking.

Moderate Most Popular
Trailhead: Booth Falls Trailhead
Parking: Free, ~30 spots (fills by 9am)
Elevation: 1,100 ft gain
Season: June - October
Get Directions View on AllTrails

Piney River Falls

6 miles RT • 600' gain • Alpine Lake

Less crowded than Booth Falls with equally impressive scenery. Trail passes Piney Lake before continuing to the falls. Longer drive filters out crowds. Bring a picnic—beautiful spots near the lake.

Moderate Less Crowded
Trailhead: Piney River Ranch
Parking: Free, large gravel lot
Elevation: 600 ft gain
Season: June - October

Challenging Adventures

Mount of the Holy Cross

12+ miles RT • 5,600' gain • 14er

Colorado's most iconic 14er with a snow cross visible on its face. Long and strenuous but views are transcendent. Start before dawn; be off summit by noon for weather. Technical experience recommended.

Strenuous 14,009'
Trailhead: Half Moon Trailhead
Parking: Free, unpaved lot (4WD recommended)
Elevation: 5,600 ft gain to 14,009'
Season: July - September
Get Directions View on AllTrails

Booth Lake

8 miles RT • 3,000' gain • Alpine Lake

Continue past Booth Falls to this stunning alpine lake in a glacial cirque. Trail steepens significantly above the falls. Bring layers—cold at 11,500' even in summer.

Strenuous 11,500'
Trailhead: Booth Falls Trailhead
Parking: Free, ~30 spots (fills by 9am)
Elevation: 3,000 ft gain
Season: July - September

Gore Range Trail

Multi-day • Backpacking • Wilderness

Multi-day backpacking along the spine of the Gore Range. Permits required for wilderness camping. Remote, rugged, and rewarding for experienced backpackers.

Multi-Day Permit Required
Trailhead: Red Sandstone Road Trailhead
Parking: Free, multiple trailhead options
Distance: 60+ miles total (section hikes available)
Season: July - September

Hiking Tips for Vail

  • Start early — Afternoon thunderstorms are common June-August. Be below treeline by 1pm.
  • Altitude matters — Vail sits at 8,150'. Give yourself time to acclimate before tackling big elevation gains.
  • Bring layers — Mornings are cool, afternoons warm, storms cold. Be prepared for all conditions.
  • Pack water — More than you think you need. The dry mountain air dehydrates quickly.
  • Parking fills early — Popular trailheads (Booth Falls especially) are full by 9am on summer weekends.
  • Check conditions — Snow lingers at higher elevations into July. Trail conditions vary by year.
