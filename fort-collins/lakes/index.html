Fort Collins Lakes

Water in the Front Range foothills

Fort Collins sits at the base of the Rockies with surprisingly good lake access. Horsetooth Reservoir is the crown jewel—a striking 6.5-mile body of water nestled against the foothills, visible from downtown. Beyond Horsetooth, several other lakes offer swimming, boating, fishing, and camping within easy reach of the city.

This guide covers where to go, what to do, and how to beat the crowds at Northern Colorado's favorite water destinations.

Horsetooth Reservoir

The Main Attraction

6.5 miles long • 1,900 acres

Horsetooth is Fort Collins' backyard lake, named for the rock formation at its south end that resembles a horse's tooth. The reservoir stretches along the Dakota Hogback with dramatic sandstone cliffs on the east shore. It's used for everything—swimming, boating, fishing, paddleboarding, and cliff jumping (unofficially). On summer weekends, it's packed; on Tuesday mornings, you'll have it nearly to yourself.

Best areas: South Bay for swimming, Satanka Bay for quiet, Inlet Bay for boat launching.

Essential

What to Do

Activities & access

Swimming: Designated beaches at South Bay and Satanka Bay. Water is cold even in summer—refreshing on a hot day, bracing if you're not expecting it.

Boating: Motorboats, sailboats, kayaks, SUPs all welcome. Rentals available at Inlet Bay Marina.

Fishing: Walleye, smallmouth bass, trout. Colorado license required.

Fees: Daily vehicle pass required ($9). Annual passes available.

Multi-Use

Other Lakes Worth Visiting

Carter Lake

1,100 acres • South of Horsetooth

A bit smaller than Horsetooth and often less crowded. Carter Lake has good camping, solid fishing (rainbow trout, largemouth bass), and a more relaxed vibe. The west shore offers dramatic views of the Rockies. Swimming is allowed in designated areas. Less developed than Horsetooth, which is part of the appeal.

Best for: Camping, fishing, and escaping Horsetooth crowds.

Less Crowded

Boyd Lake

1,700 acres • Loveland

A state park east of Loveland with excellent facilities for water sports. The beach is sandy and well-maintained, the campgrounds are good, and the wakeboarding/waterskiing is popular. Flatter setting than Horsetooth (no foothills views), but easier access and warmer water. Good for families and groups.

Best for: Families, water sports, camping with facilities.

State Park

Tips for Lake Days

  • Arrive early on weekends — Parking fills by 10am at popular spots in summer. By 8am, you're golden.
  • Weekdays are dramatically better — Same lakes, 1/10th the people. If you can swing it, go Tuesday-Thursday.
  • Bring cash for parking — Some areas have pay stations that only take cash or require exact change.
  • Water is cold — Even in August, mountain reservoir water is chilly. Wetsuits help for extended water time.
  • Altitude + sun = fast burn — At 5,000+ feet, UV is stronger. Sunscreen isn't optional.
  • Watch for afternoon storms — Summer thunderstorms roll in quickly. Get off the water if you hear thunder.
