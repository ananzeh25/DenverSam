Climbing at Garden of the Gods

Technical routes on ancient red sandstone

Garden of the Gods is one of Colorado's most unique climbing destinations—300-million-year-old red sandstone spires rising against the backdrop of Pikes Peak. The climbing here is unlike anywhere else: vertical and overhanging faces on soft, featured sandstone that demands respect and technique over raw power.

Technical climbing is allowed in the park, but with strict regulations. This guide covers what you need to know before roping up on these iconic formations.

Park Location & Access

What You Need to Know First

Permit Required

Free but mandatory

All technical climbing requires a free permit from the Garden of the Gods Visitor & Nature Center. Permits are issued same-day only, starting at 5am. You must register each person in your party and specify which formations you plan to climb. Rangers do check permits, and climbing without one can result in fines and banned access.

Permit Location: Visitor Center, 1805 N 30th St
Available: Same-day only, starting at 5:00am
Cost: Free
Phone: (719) 634-6666

The Rock Is Soft

Sandstone warnings

Garden of the Gods sandstone is notoriously soft and fragile. Holds break, protection can pull, and the rock demands finesse. After rain, the rock becomes dangerously weak—wait at least 24-48 hours after significant moisture before climbing. This isn't granite; you can't muscle your way up or trust sketchy pro.

Critical: Do not climb on wet rock. It damages the stone permanently.

Safety

Technical Climbing Only

Ropes required

Bouldering and free soloing are prohibited. All climbing must be done with ropes and protection. Chalk is allowed but discouraged—use a chalk ball rather than loose chalk, and brush holds when you leave. Bolting new routes is prohibited; all climbing follows established lines with traditional protection.

Style: Traditional climbing only. No bolting.

Trad Only

Popular Formations

The park has over 150 established routes on numerous formations. Some of the most climbed include:

North Gateway Rock

Most Popular - Beginner Friendly

The most popular formation with routes for various skill levels. The Standard Route (5.4) is often a first Garden climb for many. Views of Pikes Peak from the summit.

Coordinates: 38.8792° N, 104.8815° W
Grade Range: 5.4 - 5.10
Classic Route: Standard Route (5.4), 3 pitches

South Gateway Rock

More Technical - Less Crowded

More technical and less crowded than its northern counterpart. Steeper terrain with quality moderate to hard routes.

Coordinates: 38.8773° N, 104.8815° W
Grade Range: 5.6 - 5.11
Classic Route: West Face (5.7), 2 pitches

Kindergarten Rock

Great for Building Experience

Despite the name, offers routes from 5.0 to 5.9. Good for newer climbers building sandstone experience with shorter approaches.

Coordinates: 38.8756° N, 104.8768° W
Grade Range: 5.0 - 5.9
Best For: First-timers on sandstone

Gray Rock

Quality Moderate Routes

Good moderate routes with easier approaches. Less iconic visually but quality climbing with solid rock.

Coordinates: 38.8801° N, 104.8752° W
Grade Range: 5.5 - 5.8
Approach: 5-10 minutes from parking

Hiring a Guide

Several authorized guide services operate in the park. If you're new to sandstone climbing or Garden of the Gods specifically, a guide is highly recommended. They know the routes, the rock, and the quirks of the park system.

Recommended for:

  • First-time visitors to the park
  • Climbers without sandstone experience
  • Groups wanting to climb iconic formations safely
  • Anyone uncomfortable placing trad gear in soft rock

Guide services can arrange permits, provide all gear, and tailor the experience to your skill level. Half-day and full-day options are available.

Climbing Tips

  • Arrive early — Get permits at 5am to beat crowds and climb in cooler morning temps.
  • Wait after rain — 24-48 hours minimum. Wet sandstone is dangerous and damages the rock.
  • Bring extra gear — Cams and nuts in soft rock require larger sizes than you might expect.
  • Be tourist-aware — Millions visit the park. You'll have an audience, and the park prioritizes visitor experience.
  • Check closures — Some formations close seasonally for raptor nesting (typically Feb-July).
  • Leave no trace — Brush chalk, pack out trash, and respect this public resource.
