Frisco Restaurants

Summit County's laid-back dining scene

Frisco sits at the heart of Summit County, equidistant from Breckenridge, Copper Mountain, Keystone, and A-Basin. That central location—plus more affordable lodging than Breck—makes it a popular base camp for skiers. The town itself is charming: a walkable Main Street lined with historic buildings, independent shops, and restaurants that range from casual to surprisingly refined.

What Frisco lacks in Breckenridge's buzz, it makes up for in authenticity. These are restaurants serving locals, not just tourists. Prices are reasonable by mountain standards, waits are shorter, and the food is genuinely good. Here's where to eat.

Main Street Favorites

5th Avenue Grille

Main Street

Elevated American cuisine in a historic building. The menu changes seasonally but always features well-executed proteins—steaks, seafood, game—alongside creative sides and starters. The wine list is thoughtfully curated, and the service strikes the right balance between professional and mountain-town friendly. This is where locals go for anniversaries.

Reserve: Especially on weekends and during ski season.

Peppino's Pizza

Main Street

The local pizza institution. Peppino's has been serving Summit County since 1980, and the formula hasn't changed because it doesn't need to. New York-style pies, generous toppings, cold beer, and an atmosphere that welcomes ski boots and families equally. It's always busy for good reason. Counter service keeps things moving.

Order: A large pepperoni and a pitcher. Classic.

Greco's Pastaria

Main Street

House-made pasta in a cozy Main Street location. The menu covers Italian-American classics—lasagna, chicken parm, various pasta shapes with quality sauces—executed with care. Portions are generous, prices are fair, and the garlic bread is proper. It's the kind of place where you leave full and happy without spending a fortune.

Comfort food: Perfect for post-ski carb loading.

Vinny's Euro American

Main Street

A local favorite for more adventurous dining. The menu leans European with Italian and French influences, but the execution is distinctly personal. The chef-owner has been here for years, and regulars have their favorite dishes. It's intimate, slightly eccentric, and delivers genuine quality. BYOB-friendly with corkage.

Note: Smaller space, reservations smart.

Casual & Quick

Log Cabin Café — Breakfast institution in an actual log cabin. Massive omelets, strong coffee, reasonable prices. The line on weekend mornings is a sign of quality.

Butterhorn Bakery — The morning spot for pastries, coffee, and breakfast sandwiches. The namesake butterhorns are worth the hype.

Bagali's Pub — Proper pub food and cold beer. Good burgers, solid wings, sports on TV. Exactly what you want après-ski.

Kemosabe — Quick, affordable Mexican food. Tacos and burritos that fuel ski days without emptying your wallet.

Breweries & Bars

Outer Range Brewing — Just outside town, this brewery has become a destination for hazy IPA lovers. The taproom has mountain views and a laid-back vibe.

Highside Brewing — Main Street brewery with solid beers and a relaxed atmosphere. Good for groups.

The Moose Jaw — Dive bar with character. Cheap drinks, pool tables, locals. Classic mountain town watering hole.
