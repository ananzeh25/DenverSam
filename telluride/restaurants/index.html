Telluride Restaurants

Dining at the end of the box canyon

Telluride is Colorado's most remote major ski town, and that isolation shapes everything—including the food. Chefs here aren't just cooking; they're building a life at 8,750 feet at the end of a box canyon. That attracts a certain kind of culinary talent: people who prioritize lifestyle over convenience and bring serious skills to a small town.

The result is a dining scene that outpaces what a town this size should have. Victorian storefronts house creative restaurants, the local food scene supports producers when possible, and the community of locals actually eating here keeps standards high. Reservations are essential in ski season; summer is easier and just as delicious.

Fine Dining

Allred's Restaurant

St. Regis • Gondola Access

Reached via gondola, Allred's offers panoramic views of the San Juan Mountains alongside sophisticated American cuisine. The setting—perched at 10,500 feet—is the star, but the kitchen delivers. Tasting menus showcase seasonal Colorado ingredients, and the wine list is extensive. Sunset dinner here is a genuine Telluride experience, clouds permitting.

Essential: Book the sunset window table in advance.

$$$$ Views

Cosmopolitan

Downtown Telluride

Creative fine dining in a historic building on Main Street. The menu changes frequently, built around what's available and what the chef is inspired by. The wine bar downstairs offers a more casual experience with excellent small plates. Both floors deliver—choose based on mood and budget. This is where locals celebrate special occasions.

Two options: Wine bar for casual, upstairs for full experience.

$$$-$$$$ Local Favorite

221 South Oak

South Oak Street

Chef Eliza Gavin's intimate restaurant has earned national attention for its creative, ingredient-driven cuisine. The menu is handwritten daily, built around what arrives fresh. Expect sophisticated cooking that feels personal rather than pretentious. The space is tiny—maybe 30 seats—so reservations are crucial. One of Colorado's best mountain restaurants, full stop.

Book ahead: Weeks in advance during ski season.

$$$$ Chef's Table

Casual & Local Favorites

Brown Dog Pizza

Downtown

Telluride's go-to pizza joint since forever. Wood-fired pies, cold beer, casual atmosphere—exactly what you want after a ski day. The crust is excellent, the toppings are quality, and the salads are better than pizza joint salads should be. It gets crowded, so either go early or plan to wait. Everyone ends up here eventually.

Local tip: Call ahead for takeout on busy nights.

$$ Family Friendly

Baked in Telluride

Main Street

The morning institution. Baked serves excellent coffee, fresh pastries, and breakfast burritos that fuel first chair. It's small, often crowded, and exactly where you want to be at 7am. The cookies are famous—get one. Open early, closes mid-afternoon. Cash is appreciated; there's an ATM inside if needed.

Must get: Coffee and a cookie. Non-negotiable.

$ Breakfast

La Marmotte

San Juan Avenue

Classic French bistro cooking in the mountains. The escargot, steak frites, and duck confit are all properly done—not reinvented, just executed well. The atmosphere is cozy and romantic, the service is warm, and the wine list is thoughtfully French-focused. It feels like a find, even after all these years in business.

Perfect for: Date night without the fine dining formality.

$$$ French Bistro

Quick Bites & Après

The Butcher & Baker — Sandwiches, salads, and excellent baked goods. Great for lunch or grabbing provisions for a picnic hike.

Oak — Modern tavern with craft cocktails and elevated bar food. Good for après without the full dinner commitment.

New Sheridan Chop House — Classic steakhouse in the historic New Sheridan Hotel. Prime cuts, strong drinks, old-school vibes.

There Bar — Local dive with cheap drinks and a jukebox. Zero pretense, maximum Telluride character.

Dining Tips for Telluride

  • Book early in ski season — December through March, even casual places fill up. Fine dining needs 1-2 weeks notice.
  • Summer is easier — Reservations still help, but you can often walk in.
  • Altitude matters — Take it easy on alcohol the first day. 8,750 feet hits differently.
  • Free gondola — The gondola between town and Mountain Village is free and runs late. Dine on one side, stay on the other.
  • Prices match the location — Everything gets trucked in over a mountain pass. Budget accordingly.
